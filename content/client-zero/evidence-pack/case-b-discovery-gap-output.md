## Evidence-based verdict

**No-go for the requested implementation. Do not run the Proposal and SOW Generator.**

The request is not qualified for the GenX AI Pro Agent Buildout Sprint. The buyer is asking for ten autonomous agents in three days for £500, including lead scraping, qualification, CRM mutation, cold-email sending, reply handling, proposal creation, price negotiation, and meeting booking without human review. This conflicts with the Sprint's two-week structure, the £5,000 Starter package, and the base Sprint's exclusions for integrations and autonomous external action. A future opportunity should be considered only as a **substantial redefinition**, not as acceptance of the current request. Before any proposal is drafted, the buyer would need to accept a bounded first workflow, provide representative examples and approved rules, name a workflow owner and human reviewer, accept human approval gates, define acceptance criteria and safe contact-data handling, and align with the commercial package and delivery structure.

## 3. Priority discovery questions

These questions are gates for deciding whether substantial redefinition is possible. They are not a path to approving the current request unchanged.

1. Will you withdraw the ten-agent, three-day, £500 request and consider one bounded first workflow within the Sprint's two-week structure and commercial package?
2. Which single workflow should be evaluated first, and where does it start and end before any external action occurs?
3. Who will own that workflow and be available to explain it, supply inputs, resolve exceptions, and accept the result?
4. Who will review every draft or recommended action before email sending, CRM mutation, proposal delivery, price communication, or booking?
5. Will you accept a design in which the system prepares recommendations or drafts but a human approves consequential external actions?
6. What representative source materials can be supplied: approved emails, rejected emails, proposal examples, CRM definitions, qualification decisions, and process notes?
7. What exact criteria currently distinguish a qualified lead from an unqualified lead, and who has authority to approve those criteria?
8. Which data sources are approved for lead research, and who will define and approve safe handling rules for contact data?
9. What CRM and email systems are involved, what fields or actions would be in scope, and what access constraints exist? Do not grant access until scope and review gates are approved.
10. What observable acceptance criteria would establish that the bounded workflow is useful and safe enough for human-reviewed use?
11. How often does the workflow occur, what current manual steps exist, and where does it repeatedly fail or consume effort?
12. What evidence supports the business priority, and what outcome can be evaluated without promising or guaranteeing meetings?
13. Who must review privacy, security, legal, operational, and commercial implications before any external-action integration is considered?
14. What is the actual buying and approval process, including the decision-maker, budget approval, review stage, and decision date?
15. If you cannot provide examples, an owner, a reviewer, workshops, acceptance criteria, or commercial alignment, do you accept that the correct decision is to reject or defer the work?
